The morning commute, Kenji decided, was a dungeon that paid wages.

He boarded the 7:58 Chuo rapid at Mitaka with the rest of the drowned: students sleep-standing under headphone clouds, construction workers with knee braces, women in pinstripes doing their eyeliner in the door glass with the steadiness of surgeons. Two hundred human bodies in a steel box, every one of them breathing.

Nobody was counting.

He was. Four in, six out, tongue to palate, left hand loose around the strap, weight centered over the balls of his feet the way the physiotherapist had nagged him about for three years and he had ignored for three years because it was easier to ignore her than to fire his hips.

【DEPOSIT RECORDED: 1 QI (+10% IMPERFECT TECHNIQUE).】

The increments scrolled past with each completed cycle, and Kenji tracked them the way he tracked anything: obsessively, and with growing suspicion of the pattern.

On the platform: one qi per cycle, plus the ten percent bonus. On the train: same. But when the train hit the long curve before Yotsuya — where the rails always shriek and every body in the car sways left together like a school of fish — the window flashed.

【ENVIRONMENTAL RESONANCE: +2 QI.】

Resonance. He filed the word away and ran an experiment. He waited for the curve, held his cycle to the exact moment the car swayed, and exhaled with it instead of against it.

【RESONANT CYCLE: 4 QI (+10%).】

Four, where a quiet breath earned one. Kenji stood very still among two hundred sleeping commuters and felt something he had not felt since his first close, eleven years ago, when a client had signed on the line that was dotted. The pure administrative joy of an arbitrage.

The System — he had begun calling it that, privately, the way you name a stray — seemed to approve of the emotion. A new window unfolded, gold trim catching the fluorescent light that no one else could see.

【TECHNIQUE REFERENCE UPDATED: COMMUTER'S CYCLE (IMPERFECT).
Synchronize breath with environmental resonance. Deposit value: see ledger.
NOTE: THE PRACTITIONER IS ADVISED THAT READING WHILE STANDING IS COMMON. BREATHING WHILE AWARE IS NOT.】

"Advised," Kenji muttered. Even his hallucination had a compliance department.

* * *

The Fujimoto account was on fire in the boring way, which is to say the numbers were fine and the feelings were not. Fujimoto Logistics wanted a revised forecast by Thursday. Sawatari, his section chief, delivered this news at 9:40 a.m. while holding a printout of the old forecast against Kenji's desk like a subpoena.

"Thursday," Sawatari said. He was a wide man with the calendar-fu of a shogi champion and the interior life of a vending machine. "They're anxious about the port numbers."

"The port numbers are the port numbers," Kenji said. "They've been the same numbers since April."

"Then we'll give them the same numbers," Sawatari said, "with new anxiety."

And there it was. A knot, arriving exactly on schedule, just under Kenji's sternum — the familiar Thursday-morning knot, eleven years in the making, the one his doctor called stress and his ex-wife had called his real marriage.

Except this time, the ledger saw it.

【STAGNATION DETECTED. 3 QI WITHHELD.

REASON RECORDED: ANGER, ROUTINE, LOW-GRADE.】

Withheld. Not lost — withheld. Kenji sat through the rest of the meeting taking minutes he would never read, and every time Sawatari said the word alignment, he watched three more qi fail to post. His balance, which had climbed to 1,190 on the ride in, was frozen like a card flagged for fraud.

He thought about it through lunch, alone at his desk with an onigiri, in the open-plan quiet of two hundred people chewing in parallel. The System did not count breaths. It had never counted breaths. It counted breaths that were *present*. The ride in had earned four hundred qi because for thirty-eight minutes he had done exactly one thing, in a crowd of two hundred people doing zero things. The meeting had earned nothing, because his body was in the chair and his qi was in the argument he was rehearsing instead of having.

Cultivation, if that's what this was, wasn't a gym. It was an attendance policy.

At 1:15 p.m., in the fourth-floor bathroom, under the green flicker of a light the building manager had been meaning to fix since spring, Kenji Mori stood at the urinal of enlightenment, breathed four in and six out, and for the first time in his professional life did not think about Fujimoto Logistics.

【STAGNATION CLEARED. 3 QI RELEASED.
MERIDIAN FLOW: MINOR. FOUNDATION ESTABLISHMENT: 2%.】

Two percent. Eleven days of perfect riding, by his math, assuming no more meetings. He looked at his calendar. Eleven days of meetings.

He exhaled, aligned his hips, and went back to his desk to make the numbers agree with the anxiety. Some tribulations bill daily. You might as well breathe through them.

At 7:40 that evening, packing his bag, he caught Aizawa from risk management watching him from across the floor. She had a reputation for never looking up from her monitors, and she was looking up now — not at him, precisely. At the air about a meter in front of his chest.

Their eyes met. Aizawa returned to her monitors with the smoothness of a drawer closing.

【NOTE: THE PRACTITIONER IS NOT ALONE ON THIS TRAIN.】

Kenji stood very still, bag in hand, and read the line twice.

On the ride home, for the first cycle all day, he forgot to breathe.
