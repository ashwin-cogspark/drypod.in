Chapter 6 — The Chinpira

Two days passed. Kenji embedded himself at The Golden Goose with the patient thoroughness of a tick finding the warmest spot. Nakamura had gone from suspicious to grudgingly impressed to — by day three — actively dependent. The old accountant had reorganized two years of chaotic records into a clean, searchable system, identified three areas where the bar was hemorrhaging money (none of which were the skimming, which Kenji carefully ignored), and presented Nakamura with a recovery plan that would save the bar 400,000 yen a month.

Nakamura, who had never in his life had someone make his problems smaller, stared at the recovery plan like a dog who'd been handed a steak.

"You're hired full-time," he said. "Same rate. Cash."

Kenji bowed. "Thank you, Nakamura-san."

Behind his neutral expression, Kenji was cataloguing everything. The bar's staff — three bartenders, two servers, a bouncer named Goto who was built like a vending machine and twice as expressive. The delivery schedule — alcohol shipments on Tuesdays and Fridays, cash pickups on Thursdays. The cash pickups were the interesting part. A man in a black sedan came every Thursday at closing time, collected an envelope from Nakamura, and left without a word. The envelope contained the week's tribute to Kuroda-gumi — minus, of course, Nakamura's five percent skim.

Kenji reported all of this to Gao Qiang in the morning, over instant coffee at the low table.

Tanaka Mei's intelligence was different. Street Whisper had proven to be disturbingly effective. In forty-eight hours, she'd mapped the foot traffic patterns of three city blocks, identified the regular patrol schedule of the local chinpira crew (a group of four young men affiliated with Kuroda-gumi who collected "protection fees" from street-level businesses), and logged several regular faces worth watching.

"One more thing," Mei added. "A woman in plainclothes — I assumed plainclothes detective at first, but she's not trying to hide it. Police-issue shoes. The walk. She's been past our building three times this week."

Gao Qiang went still. Sakamoto Yui. "Describe her."

"Mid-twenties. Sharp. Takes notes on her phone when she thinks no one's looking. She's watching the building, not any specific person."

That matched. He remembered her from move-in day — the one who'd seen through the uncle story in about ten seconds flat. "Keep tracking her pattern. If she starts knocking on doors or talking to neighbors, tell me immediately."

Mei nodded. Then she turned to a fresh page in the notebook she'd started carrying everywhere, and her tone shifted from routine to something more serious.

"There's one they call Sato," she said. "Youngest of the four — nineteen, maybe twenty. The others treat him like garbage. He carries the heavy stuff, runs the errands, takes the blame when something goes wrong. Last night, one of the older ones — a guy named Hasegawa — slapped him in front of a ramen shop because Sato dropped an envelope of collected cash. Sato picked it up and didn't say a word. But I saw his face when Hasegawa walked away."

"What did you see?"

"Same thing I saw in the mirror six months ago." Mei's voice was quiet. "Someone who's given up. Someone who's still moving because stopping isn't an option, not because he wants to keep going."

Gao Qiang absorbed this. "You think he's recruitable."

"I think," Mei said, "that he's exactly the kind of person you took in. Me and Kenji-san. Broken by circumstance. Still standing."

The system agreed. When Gao Qiang focused on the name "Sato," a partial profile appeared:

---

**⚠ POTENTIAL RECRUITMENT TARGET DETECTED**

▸ **Name:** Sato Kenjiro
▸ **Age:** 19
▸ **Occupation:** Street collector (chinpira), Kuroda-gumi affiliate
▸ **Background:** Orphan. Aged out of foster care at 18. Recruited by Kuroda-gumi as muscle. Lowest rung. Regularly abused by superiors.
▸ **Latent Skills:** High pain tolerance, street navigation, physical endurance, loyalty capacity (dormant).
▸ **Suggested Class:** Fighter
▸ **Recruitment Cost:** 40 LP
▸ **Loyalty Score:** 72/100 (below threshold — requires intervention event to raise above 80)
**Note:** Loyalty Score will not reach recruitment threshold without a significant act of trust or protection. Proceed with caution — approaching a Kuroda-gumi affiliate carries inherent risk.

---

Loyalty score: 72. Threshold was 80. Eight points short. The system was telling him he couldn't just walk up and recruit this kid — he needed to *earn* it.

"How often does Hasegawa beat him?" Gao Qiang asked.

Mei didn't hesitate. "Every few days. Sometimes it's a short count, sometimes a late collection, sometimes nothing at all — just Hasegawa needing to hit someone and Sato being the closest target. Takahashi and Mori watch. They don't interfere."

"Every few days," Kenji said slowly. "That means Thursday, most likely. After the collection route, when they regroup to count the take. If the numbers are even slightly off — or if Hasegawa just needs someone to hurt — Sato takes it."

"The regroup point?" Gao Qiang asked.

"Mei's still narrowing it down," Kenji said. "Somewhere south of Block 10. A ramen shop with a back alley, probably."

Gao Qiang looked at them. His first two recruits. Kenji, the accountant who could see through any financial smokescreen. Mei, the waitress who could read a street like a book. Neither of them were fighters. Neither of them could protect him in a physical confrontation.

But they could *think*. And thinking was the weapon that mattered most right now.

"We need a third," Gao Qiang said. "Someone who can be in that moment without being traced back to us. Someone who can fight."

He checked the system. **Remaining LP: 220.** He could afford to spend, but he needed to spend wisely.

The system's territory map pulsed. A new icon had appeared on the Kabukicho grid — a small figure near the edge of the three-block zone Mei had mapped. When Gao Qiang focused on it, text appeared:

---

**WANDERING RECRUITMENT TARGET**

▸ **Name:** Kojima Haruto
▸ **Age:** 26
▸ **Occupation:** Underground fighter (unlicensed)
▸ **Background:** Former college baseball player. Career-ending shoulder injury. Lost scholarship, lost identity. Fights in illegal matches in Shinjuku basements to pay medical debt. Has not won in four matches.
▸ **Latent Skills:** Combat training (improvised), physical conditioning, pain tolerance (high), crowd psychology.
▸ **Suggested Class:** Fighter
▸ **Recruitment Cost:** 60 LP
▸ **Loyalty Score:** 83/100
**Note:** Currently at lowest point. One more loss may destroy him. Intervention window: NARROW.

**[ RECRUIT ] [ DECLINE ]**

---

An underground fighter. Losing streak. One loss from rock bottom.

Gao Qiang thought about the plan forming in his head. Sato needed a protector — someone who would step in when Hasegawa came for him. Someone physical. Someone who could fight.

And this Kojima Haruto could fight. He just couldn't win.

But winning wasn't the point. Not yet. The point was *showing up*.

"Kenji," Gao Qiang said. "Where do these underground fights happen?"

"There's a basement under a karaoke bar on Ichibangai. Fights run Thursday and Saturday nights. Low-level stuff — betting pools, no serious organization. Kuroda-gumi tolerates it because it's beneath their notice."

"Tomorrow's Thursday. I want to be there."

Kenji's face tightened. "Big Brother — if Kuroda's people see you—"

"They won't. I'm a Chinese student going to a karaoke bar. There are fifty of those on any given night in Kabukicho." He looked at the system's timer on Kojima's profile. **Intervention window: 38 hours.** "I need to see this fighter myself. I need to know if he's worth sixty points."

And there was something else — something he didn't say aloud. The locked room in his chest had opened a crack when the system mentioned Zhang Wei. He could feel the rage stirring, testing the locks, and he needed something to do with it before it found its own way out.

A fight. A fighter. A chance to see what the system's Brothers could really do.

"Tomorrow," he said. "We go to the fights."

---

The basement under the karaoke bar smelled like mold, sweat, and the particular desperation of men who had nothing left to sell but their bodies.

It was smaller than Gao Qiang had expected. A concrete room, maybe fifteen meters square, with a ring of fluorescent tubes casting prison-pale light over a central area marked by nothing more than a circle of duct tape on the floor. Thirty, maybe forty people stood around the edges — a mix of salarymen slumming it, local toughs, and a few women in short skirts holding betting slips. The air was thick with cigarette smoke and the sour-sweet smell of cheap beer.

Gao Qiang stood near the back, wearing a hoodie and a surgical mask — common enough in Tokyo that it made him nearly invisible. Kenji was at the bar, nursing a water, watching the entrance. Mei was outside, running Street Whisper on the crowd entering and leaving.

The announcer — a small man with a megaphone and a neck tattoo — called the next fight.

"Alright, alright! Next match! Kojima 'The Tank' Haruto versus — making his debut tonight — 'The Pharmacist' Yamada!"

Polite scattering of applause. The crowd was not here for names. They were here for blood.

Kojima Haruto climbed into the makeshift ring. He was big — not tall, but thick, with the dense musculature of a former athlete whose body remembered training even if his mind had forgotten hope. His shoulder — the injured one, Gao Qiang noted — was wrapped in tape but favoring slightly. His eyes were flat. Dead. The eyes of a man who had lost four fights in a row and was stepping into a fifth because the alternative was not stepping at all.

His opponent was smaller, quicker, with the jittery energy of someone on stimulants. Hence the nickname, probably.

The fight started.

It was ugly. Kojima had power — Gao Qiang could see it in the way his fists moved, the raw kinetic potential in every swing — but his shoulder betrayed him. Every left hook came a half-second too slow, and The Pharmacist was fast enough to exploit it. By the second minute, Kojima was bleeding from a cut above his eye. By the third, he was limping.

But he didn't stop. He kept swinging. Kept stepping forward. Kept eating punches that would have dropped a smarter man, because stopping meant admitting that his career, his identity, his entire life was over, and Kojima Haruto was not ready to admit that.

In the fourth minute, The Pharmacist caught him with a right hook that sent Kojima crashing to the concrete floor. The room erupted — half cheering, half groaning at lost bets. Kojima lay on the ground, chest heaving, blood pooling under his cheek.

The referee counted. One. Two. Three.

Kojima's hands pressed against the floor. His arms shook. His shoulder screamed.

Four. Five.

He got one knee under him.

Six.

The Pharmacist walked over, stood above him, and raised both fists for the crowd. Showboating. Celebrating before the count was finished.

Seven.

Kojima collapsed. His forehead hit the concrete with a sound that made Gao Qiang's stomach clench. The referee called it. The crowd roared. The Pharmacist did a lap around the duct-tape circle, grinning, high on adrenaline and the cheap glory of beating a broken man.

Gao Qiang watched Kojima's handlers — two bored-looking men in track suits — drag him out of the ring and dump him on a bench against the wall. No ice. No medical attention. Just a bucket of dirty water and a towel thrown at his chest.

Five losses. The system had said four. This made five.

**Intervention window: NARROW** was flashing red in Gao Qiang's system interface.

He looked at Kojima — bleeding, beaten, abandoned on a concrete bench in a basement full of strangers — and he didn't see a fighter. He saw his father, sitting in a prison cell in Shanghai because he'd trusted the wrong partner. He saw his mother, alone in a cramped apartment, dying while no one came. He saw Kenji in the internet cafe, empty-eyed. Mei in the alley, shaking hands holding a phone.

Broken people who chose to endure.

Gao Qiang crossed the room.
