Chapter 3 — The Community Liaison

Sakamoto Yui stood in the doorway of the empty apartment and catalogued everything in three seconds. She'd been trained for this — not at the academy, where they taught you to recite legal codes and file reports nobody read, but in the field, where the only thing between you and a dead end was your ability to see what was actually in front of you.

Two men. One Chinese, early twenties, lean, the kind of lean that came from skipping meals rather than gym membership. Pleasant face. Pleasant smile. The kind of pleasant that made her suspicious because in Kabukicho, pleasant was a weapon. The other was Japanese, fifties, gray suit, the haunted look of a man who'd been sleeping rough until very recently. The apartment was bare. No furniture, no boxes, no signs of a normal move-in.

But there were details. The younger man — the Chinese one — held himself like someone who was used to being in charge. Not arrogant. Settled. The way a good section chief stands, weight evenly distributed, eyes tracking the room without moving his head. And the older man stood behind him at an angle that said *protection* without saying it. Not a servant. Something else.

"You must be the new tenants," Sakamoto said, producing a clipboard from her tote bag. It was a prop — the real notes were in her head. "The building manager mentioned you'd signed this morning. I like to check in with new residents in the area. Kabukicho has its... quirks."

"I've noticed," Gao Qiang said. "I'm Gao Qiang. Keio University, business faculty. This is my — uncle, Murakami Kenji. He's helping me settle in."

Uncle. Sakamoto filed the lie without blinking. Different last names, no physical resemblance, and the older man hadn't flinched at the label — he'd accepted it with the smoothness of someone who'd been briefed.

"Gao Qiang-san," she said, writing nothing on her clipboard. "Chinese national?"

"Student visa. Second year. I had some housing difficulties on campus, so I found this place." He gestured at the empty room with a self-deprecating smile. "As you can see, we're still getting set up."

"Housing difficulties?"

"There was an incident. A misunderstanding about a laptop. It was resolved, but the dorm contract was already terminated." He said it matter-of-factly, without bitterness. That interested her. Most people in that situation would be angry, defensive. This kid was *narrating* — giving her the facts in a way that preempted suspicion.

Sakamoto had read the incident report. The Korean student had withdrawn the complaint, but the housing office had moved on the termination before the retraction. Sloppy work. She'd flagged it at the time, and nobody had cared. Nobody ever cared about foreign students in Kabukicho.

"And you chose this building specifically?" she asked.

"Price," Gao Qiang said simply. "I'm on scholarship. This is what I can afford."

It was plausible. It was even true, probably. But Sakamoto had been a community liaison in Kabukicho for two years, and she'd learned that the most dangerous people in this neighborhood weren't the ones who looked dangerous. They were the ones who looked exactly like this: polite, broke, and just reasonable enough to make you doubt your own instincts.

"Well," she said, capping her pen, "I'll leave you to get settled. Here's my card. If you see anything suspicious — unusual activity, people who don't belong in the building — don't hesitate to call."

She handed the card to Gao Qiang. Their fingers touched briefly. His hand was warm. Steady.

"Thank you, Officer Sakamoto," he said. "I appreciate the welcome."

She nodded and left. Walked down four flights of stairs with her clipboard under her arm, stepped out into the yakitori-scented street, and didn't look back.

Only when she was two blocks away did she take out her phone and dial.

"Sergeant Takeda. It's Sakamoto. The new tenants in the Kabukicho building — fourth floor. One's a Chinese student, Keio business. The other's a middle-aged Japanese man. They're claiming uncle and nephew."

A pause on the other end. "And?"

"And the building manager flagged it because Kuroda's people have been asking about that unit. Someone paid the rent three months in advance. Cash."

Another pause. "You think they're connected?"

"I think," Sakamoto said, "that a Chinese student with a terminated dorm contract doesn't pay 270,000 yen in cash three months upfront on a scholarship. Something's off."

"Keep an eye on it. Low priority. We've got bigger problems."

The line went dead. Sakamoto pocketed her phone and kept walking, her mind already chewing on the puzzle.

She didn't know it yet, but she'd just taken her first step into Gao Qiang's orbit. And she wouldn't be walking out.

---

Back in the apartment, Gao Qiang leaned against the wall and exhaled.

"She's not just a community liaison," he said.

Kenji shook his head. "No. The way she scanned the room — that's investigative training. And she didn't write anything on that clipboard. She was memorizing."

"The system flagged her. Said she's been assigned to monitor this building because of Kuroda-gumi activity."

"Then Kuroda's people have eyes on this building," Kenji said, and his voice carried the flat certainty of a man who understood exactly how badly that could go. "We need to be careful."

"Careful is for people with margin." Gao Qiang pulled out his notebook and flipped to a fresh page. "We don't have margin. We have momentum. Kenji — how soon can you get to The Golden Goose?"

"Tonight, if you want."

"Go. And be careful anyway. Momentum without control is just falling."

Kenji bowed slightly — a gesture that was becoming reflex — and left.

Gao Qiang was alone. He sat on the floor of the empty living room, back against the wall, and opened the system.

Gao Qiang scrolled. The system had a **Shop** section, currently locked behind "Brotherhood Level 2" — requiring 3 brothers. There was a **Territory Map** that showed Kabukicho as a grey grid with a single pin on his building. And there was a **Lore Database** with multiple locked entries, the titles of which made his fingers itch:

- Entry 002: The Iron Rule Explained *(Locked — requires 5 Brothers)*
- Entry 003: The Origin of the System *(Locked — requires 10 Brothers)*
- Entry 004: The Final Purpose *(Locked — requires Brotherhood Level 5)*
- Entry 005: The Three betrayals *(Locked — Story Progression)*

He also found his own ability profile. Big Brother Aura was no longer listed as "???" — it had a proper entry now:

▸ **Big Brother Aura** (Passive, Level 1) — Projects a subtle sense of trustworthiness and authority. More effective on individuals with lower willpower or in states of emotional uncertainty. Reduced effectiveness against trained observers or individuals with strong pre-existing suspicion.

That explained the landlord. It also meant the police officer — Sakamoto Yui, sharp-eyed and already suspicious — would be largely immune. Good to know. The system had limits. He needed to learn what they were.

He closed the interface. Too many locked doors. He needed more brothers, more LP, more information.

His phone buzzed. A text from an unfamiliar number.

**Gao Qiang-san, this is Park Soo-jin from your Corporate Strategy seminar. Professor Yamada moved the group project deadline to Friday. You're in Group 7. Meeting tomorrow at 3pm in the library. Don't be late — I'm not carrying dead weight.**

No greeting. No pleasantries. Just orders.

Gao Qiang almost laughed. Almost.

He typed back: **Noted. See you there.**

Then he lay down on the bare floor — no futon, no blanket, just the cool wood under his back — and stared at the ceiling. Tomorrow: class, group project, and whatever Kenji found at The Golden Goose.

Tonight: the ceiling, and the rage coiled beneath his ribs like a sleeping animal.

He closed his eyes and didn't dream.
