Chapter 8 — Thursday

Thursday arrived the way Thursdays always do in Kabukicho — hungover from Wednesday and already bracing for Friday.

Gao Qiang spent the morning at Keio, sitting through a lecture on mergers and acquisitions that he could have taught himself, taking notes with one hand while his other hand — the one hidden beneath the desk — scrolled through the system's newly unlocked Shop.

The Shop was modest but promising:

**Brotherhood Shop — Level 2**

| Item | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Healing Salve (Minor) | 20 LP | Accelerates recovery of one Brother's injuries by 50% |
| Safe House Upgrade (Lv1→Lv2) | 50 LP | Adds hidden room, reinforced door, basic surveillance |
| Fake ID Template | 40 LP | Generates convincing identification for one Brother |
| Burner Phone Pack (x5) | 15 LP | Untraceable communication for the Brotherhood |
| Combat Primer: Basic | 30 LP | Temporarily boosts a Brother's fighting stats by 15% for one encounter |

He bought the Healing Salve for Kojima (20 LP), the Burner Phone Pack (15 LP), and the Combat Primer: Basic (30 LP). Total: 65 LP. Remaining: 195.

The Healing Salve appeared in his bag — literally materialized, a small ceramic jar with a label written in characters that shimmered and then settled into ordinary Japanese. He didn't question it. The system had its rules, and *don't question the inventory* was clearly one of them.

Before closing the interface, he noticed a new entry under his personal abilities — one he hadn't seen before the Brotherhood Level 2 up:

▸ **Audio Enhancement** (Passive, Level 2) — Enhances hearing within Brotherhood territory or during active missions. Detects conversation at distances up to 100 meters. Activates automatically when Brothers or mission targets are within range.

He studied it for a moment. The system was still giving him things he hadn't asked for. Useful, but he'd need to figure out the full scope of what Brotherhood Level 2 had unlocked later.

At noon, he met Park Soo-jin in the library for the group project. Ogawa was eleven minutes late and arrived with a crepe.

"You're late," Park Soo-jin said.

"Traffic."

"You live on campus."

"Campus traffic." Ogawa sat down and pulled out a single sheet of paper. "Industry overview. I did it. Don't look so shocked."

Park Soo-jin looked shocked. She covered it well, but Gao Qiang caught the microexpression — a brief widening of the eyes that said *this idiot actually did the work.*

She turned to Gao Qiang. "Financial analysis?"

Gao Qiang placed his section on the table. Twelve pages. Handwritten — he didn't have a printer — but organized with the kind of precision that came from a lifetime of balance sheets. Every number cross-referenced. Every anomaly flagged. And buried in the middle, under a section titled "Irregular Cash Flow Patterns in Overseas Consulting Operations," was the hook — the connection between Tanaka Heavy Industries' phantom consulting fees and the money trail that led, eventually, to Zhang Wei.

Park Soo-jin read it in silence. Her eyes moved faster than her fingers could turn pages. When she reached the cash flow section, she stopped.

"This is..." She looked up. "Where did you learn to read financial statements like this?"

"My father taught me."

"Your father is an accountant?"

"My father is in prison." Gao Qiang's voice was flat. Not angry. Not sad. Just factual. "He was framed by his business partner. I grew up reading the evidence."

Silence. Ogawa stopped eating his crepe. Park Soo-jin's expression performed a complicated maneuver — surprise, discomfort, a flicker of something that might have been respect, and then a controlled neutral that was almost professional.

"I'm sorry," she said.

"Don't be. It's why I'm good at this."

She held his gaze for a long moment. Something passed between them — not romantic, not yet. Recognition. The acknowledgment of a shared quality: *I know what it costs to be this sharp, because I paid for it too.*

"This section on consulting fees," she said, tapping the page. "You're suggesting there's something irregular about the overseas partnerships."

"I'm suggesting the growth rate is inconsistent with the revenue return. Two point three billion yen in consulting fees with no corresponding increase in service income, market presence, or IP development. That's not a partnership. That's a pipe."

"A pipe," Ogawa said, mouth full of crepe. "Like — a drug pipe?"

"Like a money pipe," Gao Qiang said. "Cash goes in one end and disappears. Where it comes out is the interesting question."

Park Soo-jin's eyes narrowed. She was sharp. Sharper than Gao Qiang had expected. In another life, she might have been a system recruit herself.

"We can't include that in the academic report," she said. "It's speculative. We don't have evidence."

"No," Gao Qiang agreed. "We present the numbers. The irregularity speaks for itself. We note it as a risk factor and move on."

"And the real question — where the money goes — stays with you."

It wasn't an accusation. It was an observation. Park Soo-jin had just demonstrated that she could see exactly what Gao Qiang was doing: using a university project as cover for private intelligence gathering. And she wasn't stopping him. She was *noting* it.

"For now," he said.

She held his gaze for another beat, then gathered the pages into a neat stack.

"Thursday noon was the deadline. You're early. Good work, Gao Qiang."

"Thank you, Park-san."

"Soo-jin," she corrected. "If we're going to spend the next three years in the same faculty, the formality is going to get tedious."

"Soo-jin," he repeated.

She stood, tucked the stack under her arm, and left without another word. The crepe wrapper was still on the table. Ogawa looked at Gao Qiang with wide eyes.

"Did she just... give you permission to call her by her first name?"

"I think so."

"That's — dude, she doesn't even let *me* call her Soo-jin. She calls me 'Ogawa' like it's a medical condition." He shook his head. "What the hell did you put in that financial report?"

"The truth."

"Must be nice," Ogawa muttered, "having the truth be impressive."

Gao Qiang almost liked him. Almost.

But long after Ogawa had gathered his things and wandered off, Gao Qiang was still looking at the door Soo-jin had left through. She'd seen exactly what he was doing. Seen it, named it, and let it pass. That wasn't naivety — nothing about Park Soo-jin was naive. It was something else. The "controlled neutral" when he'd mentioned his father — that wasn't academic composure. It was practiced. The way someone looks when they've learned not to react because reactions are dangerous.

He didn't know her story. But he recognized the performance. Everyone with a locked room in their chest learned the same moves eventually.

Soo-jin was either a future ally or a future problem. He'd find out which soon enough.

The week had been about survival.

Three days in, Kenji had come to him with a proposal.

"Nakamura's skim," he'd said, pouring tea. "It's systematic — about five percent of the weekly take. If I adjust the reconciliation by a few thousand yen each shift, it comes from his pocket, not Kuroda's. He won't notice because he doesn't look at the details — that's why he hired me."

"How much?"

"Fifteen to twenty thousand a week. Off the books. Clean."

Gao Qiang had considered it for about three seconds. They were stealing from a man who was stealing from the yakuza. The moral math was simple.

"Do it," he said. "Start small. Stay invisible."

Kenji had nodded. "Already started. I didn't want to ask first."

Good accountant. Better brother.

He closed the interface and bought four onigiri.

Evening. The apartment.

The Brotherhood gathered around the low table for what Gao Qiang had come to think of as the war council. Kenji had tea. Mei had her notebook. Kojima had the Healing Salve applied to his face, and the swelling had visibly reduced — not gone, but enough that he could open both eyes and speak without wincing.

The system's Combat Primer: Basic was a thin pamphlet that had appeared in Gao Qiang's bag alongside the salve. It contained diagrams — pressure points, joint locks, basic defensive footwork — and when Gao Qiang handed it to Kojima, the fighter's eyes lit up like a kid given a comic book.

"This is systematized," Kojima said, flipping through the pages. "These aren't street fighting techniques. This is structured combat theory."

"The system provides," Gao Qiang said. "Study it. Tonight's the night."

He laid out the plan.

The Thursday tribute pickup at The Golden Goose happened at closing time — around 1 AM. Nakamura handed the envelope to the man in the black sedan. Simultaneously, the chinpira crew — Hasegawa and his three men, including Sato — collected street-level protection fees from businesses in a four-block radius.

Mei's surveillance had pinpointed their route and schedule. They moved clockwise, starting at the north end of Block 7 and ending at the south end of Block 10 by midnight. After collections, they regrouped at a ramen shop called **Menya Kuro** to count the take before delivering it to the sedan driver.

"Kojima," Gao Qiang said. "Your job is straightforward. Get to Menya Kuro before midnight. Find a position in the back alley with a sightline to the regroup point. You don't engage unless Sato is in danger."

"Wait for Hasegawa to snap," Kojima said. It wasn't a question.

"Mei's surveillance says it happens every few days. Thursday nights are worst — fresh collection money, numbers to argue about. Hasegawa needs a target, and Sato is always the nearest one."

"And when he does?"

"You stop it. Hasegawa only. You're not there to fight Kuroda-gumi — you're there to protect someone who can't protect himself. After that, you tell Sato: 'If you want to stop taking hits for people who don't care about you, there's a place where you don't have to.' Then you walk away."

"Big Brother," Kenji said carefully. "If Kojima is seen fighting a Kuroda chinpira—"

"He fights *Hasegawa*. One man. Someone new, with no known affiliation." Gao Qiang looked at each of them. "The goal tonight is simple. One objective: Sato. We do it clean."

Mei nodded. "A ghost."

"That narrative does more work than ten fighters. Let the streets do the talking."

Kojima cracked his neck. "How do you know he'll come find us?"

Gao Qiang looked at Mei, then at Kenji, then at Kojima. "Because that's how I found all of you."

Midnight.

The streets of Kabukicho hummed. Gao Qiang stood on the rooftop of his apartment building — four stories up, just high enough to see the blocks below without being seen. The system's territory map overlaid his vision like augmented reality, showing moving dots for his deployed Brothers.

Kenji's dot was at The Golden Goose. Mei's dot was two blocks east, positioned on a bench near a 24-hour laundromat with a sightline to Menya Kuro's back alley. Kojima's dot was in the alley itself, hidden in a recessed doorway behind a dumpster that smelled like old cooking oil and regret.

The chinpira crew moved through the streets below. Four dots on the system map, labeled with names Mei had provided: **Hasegawa** (red — threat), **Takahashi**, **Mori**, and **Sato** (yellow — target). They were on schedule. Block 8, then 9, then 10.

At 12:35 AM, Mei's report came through the burner phone.

*Chinpira crew entering Menya Kuro. All four. Starting count in the back alley. Kojima is in position behind the dumpster. Standing by.*

Gao Qiang watched from the rooftop as the four dots clustered together behind Menya Kuro. The sedan driver's dot sat in a black car half a block south, engine idling.

No signal needed. Kojima would act when the moment came.

He waited.

Mei confirmed seconds later: *They're recounting. Takahashi and Mori are arguing about the tally. Sato is just kneeling there. Looks like he already knows he's going to get blamed.*

At 12:50 AM, the sedan driver walked to the alley, collected the envelopes, and returned to his car. He sat there for four minutes, counting. Then his brake lights flashed — a phone call.

At 12:55 AM, Hasegawa's dot stopped moving. The other three continued to the regroup point. Hasegawa stayed behind, pulling out his phone, his body language visible even from four stories up — the tight shoulders, the sharp gestures of a man receiving bad news and looking for someone to blame.

The shortage hadn't come from the brotherhood. Gao Qiang hadn't touched the collection money. Someone inside Hasegawa's own crew was skimming — and Sato was about to pay for it.

He filed it away. A chinpira crew with a thief in its ranks was instability, and instability was leverage. But tonight, leverage could wait. Tonight, a kid was about to get beaten for something he didn't do.

At 1:10 AM, Hasegawa arrived at Menya Kuro. He was moving fast, and the other three were already there, standing in the back alley. Sato was on his knees, holding a stack of envelopes, when Hasegawa grabbed him by the collar and hauled him upright.

The audio enhancement kicked in — the Level 2 passive he'd spotted in the shop this morning. He hadn't expected it to work from four stories up, but Hasegawa's voice carried on the night air, clear as a phone call:

"...thirty thousand short... you think I'm stupid... you think Kuroda-san won't notice..."

Sato's response was too quiet to hear. But his body language was clear: hands up, palms out, the universal gesture of *it wasn't me, please believe me.* Hasegawa didn't believe him. Hasegawa wasn't interested in believing him.

The first punch caught Sato in the stomach. He doubled over. The second — a backhand — sent him reeling into the wall. Takahashi and Mori watched. They didn't intervene. This was routine. Sato was the punching bag, and punching bags didn't get rescued.

Hasegawa pulled Sato up by the hair.

"Where's the money, you little—"

"Oi."

The voice came from the shadows behind the dumpster. Deep. Calm. Carrying the particular authority of someone who was not afraid of the dark or the men in it.

Hasegawa turned. Kojima Haruto stepped out of the doorway.

He was still battered — the Healing Salve had reduced the swelling but not erased it. His knuckles were wrapped in tape. His eyes, both of them open now, held the steady, unblinking focus of a man who had been beaten five times in a row and decided that he'd had enough of being the one on the ground.

"Who the f—" Hasegawa started.

"The kid didn't steal your money," Kojima said. His voice was unhurried. Almost casual. "But you already know that, don't you? You just need someone to hit so you can feel like you're in control. Pick on someone your own size."

Hasegawa released Sato. His hand went to his waistband — a knife, Gao Qiang noted, watching from the rooftop. Not a gun. Small mercy.

"You know who I work for?" Hasegawa's voice had shifted from rage to threat. "Kuroda-gumi. You touch me, and every man in this neighborhood will be looking for you."

"Good," Kojima said. "Then they'll know who beat you."

Hasegawa lunged.

The Combat Primer had been a thin pamphlet. But the knowledge it contained — pressure points, joint locks, redirection of force — had settled into Kojima's body with the system's characteristic thoroughness. He didn't fight like a street brawler anymore. He fought like a man who had been given a blueprint for violence and had the physical strength to execute it.

Hasegawa's knife hand was intercepted mid-thrust, redirected into the brick wall with a wet crack. Hasegawa screamed. Kojima twisted the wrist, stripped the knife, and swept Hasegawa's legs in a single fluid motion. The chinpira hit the ground hard, his wrist bent at an angle that wrists were not designed to achieve.

Takahashi and Mori didn't move. They weren't paid enough for this.

Kojima crouched beside the moaning Hasegawa. He pressed the confiscated knife into the wall beside his head, pinning a section of his jacket to the brick.

"Tell Kuroda's people to check their own count before beating their own men. The money didn't walk away on its own — someone higher up lost it, not a kid on his knees." He stood. "And if I ever see you touch this kid again, I won't be as gentle."

He turned to Sato, who was sitting against the wall, holding his stomach, staring at Kojima with an expression that was equal parts shock, gratitude, and something deeper — the dawning awareness that someone, for the first time in his miserable life, had stood up for him.

"Hey, kid," Kojima said. "You don't have to keep taking hits for people who don't care about you. There's a place where you don't have to do that."

He didn't wait for a response. He turned and walked out of the alley, disappearing into the neon-lit streets of Kabukicho like he'd never been there.

Sato sat in the alley for a long time after that. Takahashi and Mori helped Hasegawa to his feet and disappeared. The night settled. The neon buzzed. And somewhere in the city, the system chimed.

Gao Qiang lowered himself from the rooftop edge and took a breath. Below, the streets were already absorbing the violence — Kabukicho was efficient that way. A beaten chinpira, a mysterious protector, a kid left alone in an alley. Tomorrow, no one would remember. Tonight, it was the most important thing that had happened in Sato Kenjiro's life.

His phone buzzed. A message from Mei:

*He's not moving. He's just sitting there. But he's not crying either. He's thinking. He's going to come.*

Gao Qiang typed back: *Then we wait.*

He went downstairs to the apartment. Kenji was already there, reporting that the night at The Golden Goose had been uneventful — Nakamura had prepared the envelope as usual, handed it to the sedan driver, and returned to the bar. No panic. No suspicion. Kenji had spent the evening calmly reconciling receipts while the real chaos unfolded four blocks away.

Kojima arrived twenty minutes later, knuckles freshly taped, with a look of grim satisfaction on his healing face.

"How's the shoulder?" Gao Qiang asked.

"Honestly? Better than it's been in two years." Kojima flexed it experimentally. "Whatever the system did — the healing salve, the combat primer, bringing me in — it's like my body remembers how to work again."

"The system doesn't just take in fighters. It fixes what was broken."

"Big Brother," Kojima said, and his voice was serious now, "I've been broken for three years. If this is what fixed feels like, I'm never going back."

Gao Qiang nodded. He believed him. The system's Iron Rule ensured it — brothers didn't leave, didn't betray, didn't hoard. But the loyalty wasn't forced. It was earned. Every brother in this room had been given a choice, and every one of them had chosen to stay.

That was the system's true power. Not the abilities or the points or the floating interface. The power was *trust* — and trust was the one currency that multiplied when you spent it.

They set up the fourth futon. Kenji had been right — they'd have a fourth soon. The bowls were already waiting.

At 3:17 AM, there was a knock at the door.

Gao Qiang opened it.

Sato Kenjiro stood in the hallway. Nineteen years old. Black eye forming. Bruised ribs. Wearing a jacket that didn't fit over a T-shirt that hadn't been washed in two days. He was shaking — not from cold, not from fear. From the effort of standing at a stranger's door at three in the morning because a man he'd never met had told him he didn't have to take hits anymore.

"I..." Sato's voice cracked. "The man in the alley. He said there was a place."

"There is." Gao Qiang stepped aside. "Come in."

Sato crossed the threshold.

And the system sang.

The notification faded. Gao Qiang let himself feel it for a moment — the warmth of four brothers under one roof, the system's quiet approval humming behind his eyes.

Then the deadline pulled him back.

Three days. Three days to gain operational control of a bar that sat inside Kuroda-gumi's territory, run by a man who had been skimming from the yakuza for two years. Nakamura was vulnerable — Kenji had the evidence. But the Menya Kuro shortage had shown that the Kuroda operation was messier than expected. Instability cut both ways. Gao Qiang needed a way in that didn't rely on chaos he couldn't control.

He looked around the apartment. Kenji was making tea. Mei was showing Sato where the bathroom was. Kojima was studying the Combat Primer by the light of his phone.

Four people. A manager embedded in the target, an informant who could read the streets, a fighter who'd already drawn blood inside Kuroda's territory, and a former insider who knew how the whole operation worked from the ground up. The pieces were better than they'd been a week ago. But pieces weren't a plan.

Tomorrow. They'd figure it out tomorrow.

"Together," Gao Qiang said, quiet enough that only the room could hear.

No one responded. They didn't need to.
