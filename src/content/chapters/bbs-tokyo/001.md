Chapter 1 — The Weight of Eleven Million

The Narita Express pulled into Tokyo Station at 6:47 in the morning, and Gao Qiang's stomach had been growling since Chiba.

He stepped onto the platform with a single suitcase — the black one with the broken wheel that his mother had bought at a market in Pudong three years ago, back when the Qiang family still had money, back when his father wasn't rotting in Qingpu Prison, back when his mother was still alive to buy anything at all.

The suitcase screeched against the tile floor like a dying cat. A Japanese businessman in a charcoal suit glanced down at it, then at Gao Qiang's face, and his expression performed a familiar choreography: surprise, assessment, and then that particular variety of dismissal that Japanese people reserved for poor Chinese. Not aggressive. Worse. Polite. The kind that smiled while closing every door.

Gao Qiang was used to it.

He had 340,000 yen in his bank account. That was the remainder of his scholarship disbursement after the Keio University international student housing office had — in his second week — accused him of stealing a laptop from a Korean exchange student. The laptop had been found two days later in the lost-and-found, left behind by its actual owner at a Lawson's. But the accusation had done its work. His dorm contract was terminated. No hearing. No appeal. The housing coordinator, a thin woman with reading glasses who never looked him in the eye, had explained it with the mechanical precision of a train announcement: *"We take the comfort of all residents very seriously. Please understand."*

Understand. The great Japanese non-apology. Not *we're sorry*. Not *we made a mistake*. *Please understand.* Understand that you don't belong here. Understand that the rules are different for you. Understand that you'll sleep in a manga cafe tonight, and tomorrow night, and every night until you figure something out.

He'd figured something out. Sort of.

Gao Qiang navigated the station with the purposeful speed of a man who had memorized his route. Four transfers. Shinjuku. Then a fifteen-minute walk to an internet cafe called **Net Paradise 24** in Kabukicho, where he'd been living for the past eleven days in a booth barely large enough to lie down in, subsisting on instant ramen and the cafe's free soft-serve ice cream, studying for classes he could barely attend because showing up to Keio's business faculty in the same three shirts had become its own form of social suicide.

The irony was that he was good at this. Business. Strategy. Resource allocation. His father had taught him before the fall — Gao Qiang had been reading balance sheets since he was fourteen. He could see the bones of a company the way a doctor could see the bones of a body through an X-ray. Where the money flowed. Where it leaked. Where it was being stolen.

That last one was personal.

Zhang Wei. The name sat in his chest like a swallowed coal. His father's partner. The man who had orchestrated the embezzlement scheme that collapsed Qiang Pharmaceuticals, then fled to Japan with 200 million yuan and the cooperation of corrupt officials on both sides of the East China Sea. Zhang Wei was here. Somewhere in Tokyo. Living fat on stolen money while Gao Qiang's father served eighteen years and his mother had died of a stroke in a cramped apartment she couldn't afford, alone, because Gao Qiang had been at school and couldn't get to her in time.

He was here on a scholarship. But the scholarship was cover. He was here to find Zhang Wei.

His father's pharmaceutical company had dealt with Japanese firms — partnerships, licensing, the kind of deal-making that required English on both sides but went smoother when someone on your team actually spoke the language. Gao Qiang had started studying Japanese at fifteen because his father mentioned it once, offhand, at dinner. The kind of boy who collected skills like other kids collected trading cards, he'd passed N1 by eighteen. When everything fell apart, the language was one of the few things that survived. The accent was the last stubborn piece of Shanghai he couldn't shake.

And then?

He didn't know. He was twenty-two years old, broke, homeless in a foreign country, sleeping in a booth that smelled like cigarette smoke and desperation. He didn't have a plan beyond *find him*. He didn't have resources. He didn't have allies.

He had a broken suitcase and 340,000 yen and a rage that he kept in a locked room inside his chest, because if he ever opened that door, it would swallow him whole.

The Net Paradise 24 was on the third floor of a narrow building wedged between a love hotel and a pachinko parlor. The elevator smelled like cheap cologne and aging carpet. Gao Qiang rode it in silence, counting the floors in his head. Third floor. Left turn. Past the rows of booths to the back corner, number 47, his home.

He swiped his membership card at the entrance. The girl at the counter — pink hair, nose ring, bored eyes — nodded without looking up. He passed the drink bar, grabbed a canned coffee, and made his way to his booth.

There was someone in it.

A man. Maybe fifty. Gray suit, rumpled, with the look of someone who had been wearing it for three days straight. He was slumped against the partition, head bowed, hands limp in his lap. A briefcase sat at his feet. The screen displayed a job listing website — page after page of rejected applications.

Gao Qiang stopped.

"Excuse me. This is my booth."

The man looked up. His eyes were the worst part. Not sad — past sad. Empty. The kind of empty that comes after you've been fired from a company you gave twenty-five years to, after your wife has left and taken the kids, after the apartment is gone and the bridges are all burned and you're sleeping in an internet cafe because the only other option is a bridge in a more literal sense.

"I'm sorry," the man said. His voice was hoarse. "I didn't — I'll move."

He started to gather himself. Slowly. Like every joint hurt.

Gao Qiang watched him. And something stirred in that locked room in his chest. Not rage. Something else. Recognition.

"Wait," Gao Qiang said. "What's your name?"

The man paused, half-risen. "Why?"

"Just tell me."

"...Kenji. Murakami Kenji."

"How long since you've eaten?"

Kenji blinked. The question seemed to short-circuit something in him. "I... yesterday. No. The day before."

Gao Qiang set his canned coffee on the desk. He reached into his pocket and pulled out a 1,000-yen coin. Then he looked at it. Looked at the man. Looked at the coin again.

It was stupid. He needed every yen. He was one bad week away from being exactly where this man was sitting.

He handed Kenji the coin.

"Get a bento from the Lawson's downstairs. And don't argue."

Kenji stared at the coin. Then at Gao Qiang. His mouth opened, closed, opened again.

"Why?" he asked.

Gao Qiang didn't have an answer that made sense. He shrugged. "You looked like you needed it more than me."

Kenji took the coin. His hand trembled.

Then the world broke.

---

It started with a sound that wasn't a sound. A chime, but inside his skull — bright, crystalline, like a bell struck in an empty cathedral. Gao Qiang staggered, grabbing the edge of the booth partition.

A window appeared in the air in front of him.

Not a physical window. Not a screen. It was *there*, floating at arm's length, translucent, glowing with a soft blue light that only he could see. Text materialized on it, character by character, as if typed by invisible hands:

---

**「 BIG BROTHER SYSTEM v1.0 」**

**Initializing...**

**Host detected: GAO QIANG**
**Age:** 22
**Status:** Destitute
**Location:** Kabukicho, Shinjuku, Tokyo
**Loyalty Points (LP):** 100

**✦ SYSTEM ACTIVATED ✦**

**Condition met: Genuine act of selfless generosity while in a state of personal poverty.**

**Welcome, Big Brother.**

---

Gao Qiang's mouth went dry.

"What the hell," he whispered.

Kenji looked up from the coin in his hand. "What?"

"Nothing. I — nothing."

The window continued:

---

**SYSTEM OVERVIEW:**

The Big Brother System rewards those who build loyalty through action, not words.

**Core Mechanic:** Invest resources → Recruit Brothers → Brothers generate income and influence → Reinvest to expand your Brotherhood.

**THE IRON RULE:** Brothers earn. The Big Brother spends. No brother takes a single yen home. All income generated by your Brotherhood flows to you for redistribution.

**Recruitment:** Spend Loyalty Points to recruit individuals whose loyalty score exceeds the threshold. Once recruited, a Brother is bound to you until release or death.

**Brother Classes:** Fighter, Informant, Manager, Infiltrator, Entertainer, Medic, Tech.

**First Recruitment Available:**

▸ **Murakami Kenji**
▸ **Age:** 52
▸ **Former Occupation:** Senior Accountant, Tanaka Heavy Industries
▸ **Reason for Status:** Wrongfully terminated after exposing financial fraud. Blacklisted by industry. Divorced. Homeless.
▸ **Latent Skills:** Advanced financial analysis, corporate accounting, audit trail reconstruction, intimidation resistance (High).
▸ **Suggested Class:** Manager
▸ **Recruitment Cost:** 50 LP
▸ **Loyalty Score:** 94/100 (triggered by your act of generosity)

**[ RECRUIT ] [ DECLINE ]**

---

Gao Qiang stared at the window. Then at Kenji, who was still standing there with the 1,000-yen coin, looking confused and a little frightened by the way the young Chinese man was staring into empty space with wide eyes.

"Murakami-san," Gao Qiang said slowly. "You worked at Tanaka Heavy Industries?"

Kenji flinched. "How do you —"

"Did you expose a financial fraud and get fired for it?"

The color drained from Kenji's face. "Who told you that? Who *are* you?"

Gao Qiang looked back at the floating window. **Recruitment Cost: 50 LP.** He had 100. Half his points on a fifty-two-year-old homeless accountant he'd met five minutes ago.

Every business instinct his father had taught him screamed caution. Diversify. Don't bet half your capital on a single asset. Wait for better information.

But another voice — older, deeper, the voice that had made him give away his last 1,000 yen to a stranger — said something different.

*This man was destroyed for telling the truth. He has nothing left. And he has skills I need.*

Gao Qiang reached out and pressed **[ RECRUIT ]**.

The window flashed gold. Kenji stiffened, gasped, and his eyes went wide — pupils dilating, a shudder running through his body like an electric current. The 1,000-yen coin fell from his hand and rolled across the floor, forgotten.

For three seconds, nothing happened.

Then Kenji exhaled. And when he looked up at Gao Qiang, his eyes were different. Not empty anymore. Focused. Warm. *Certain.*

"Gao... Qiang-san?" Kenji said. His voice was steady now. Clear.

"Yeah."

Kenji straightened his rumpled suit jacket. Ran a hand through his disheveled gray hair. And then he bowed — a deep, formal bow, the kind given to employers, to benefactors, to people who had saved your life.

"I understand now," Kenji said. "I don't know how to explain it, but... I understand. You're my Big Brother. And I will not let you down."

The system window updated:

---

**✦ RECRUITMENT SUCCESSFUL ✦**

**Murakami Kenji** has joined your Brotherhood.

**Class:** Manager (Level 1)
**Loyalty:** 97/100 (Bond Strengthened)
**Special Ability Unlocked:** 「 Paper Trail 」 — Kenji can identify financial irregularities in any business within 24 hours of access.

**Current Brotherhood:** 1 member
**Remaining LP:** 50

**NEW MISSION AVAILABLE:**

**「 First Night 」**
*Objective: Secure permanent housing for your Brotherhood within 72 hours.*
*Reward: 200 LP, Blueprint: Safe House (Level 1)*
*Failure: -50 LP, Brotherhood morale decrease*

---

Gao Qiang read the mission. Permanent housing. With 340,000 yen and a homeless accountant.

He looked at Kenji, who was studying him with quiet, unwavering devotion.

"Kenji," Gao Qiang said. "How do you feel about helping me run an empire?"

Kenji's mouth twitched. For the first time in what was probably months, something that might have been a smile appeared on his weathered face.

"I feel like that's exactly what I was made for, Big Brother."
