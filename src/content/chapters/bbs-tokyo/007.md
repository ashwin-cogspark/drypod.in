Chapter 7 — The Tank

The handler looked up as Gao Qiang approached — a track-suited man with a shaved head and the vacant expression of someone paid by the hour to not care.

"He's done for the night," the handler said. "No post-fight selfies."

"I'm not here for a photo." Gao Qiang crouched beside the bench. Kojima Haruto was conscious — barely. His left eye was swollen shut, blood seeping from a cut above his brow and another on his lip. His breathing was ragged but even. A man who knew how to take a beating.

"Kojima-san."

One eye — the right, still functional — tracked toward Gao Qiang's voice. Confusion. Wariness. The particular suspicion of a man who had been approached by strangers in basements before and it had never ended well.

"Who..." Kojima's voice was a rasp. "The hell are you."

"Someone who just watched you get knocked down seven times and get up six."

"Seven out of seven, actually. You missed the first round. I was faster then." He tried to laugh. It came out as a wet cough.

"You didn't quit."

"Quitting costs extra." Kojima closed his eye. "Who sent you? Debt collector? Because if you're here about the medical bills, you can get in line. It wraps around the block."

"No one sent me. I'm here to offer you a job."

The eye opened again. Sharper this time. "A job."

"I need someone who can take a punch. You're the most durable man in this room."

"You want a bodyguard? Buddy, I just lost five in a row. You can do better."

"I don't need a winner." Gao Qiang kept his voice low, steady. The same voice he'd used on Mei in the alley. The same voice he'd used on Kenji with the 1,000-yen coin. "I need someone who gets back up. Winning is a skill. Getting back up is character. I can teach skill. I can't teach character."

Kojima stared at him with his one good eye. The basement noise faded to a distant hum — the announcer calling the next fight, the crowd murmuring, the clink of betting coins. None of it reached them.

"Why me?" Kojima asked. And the question wasn't suspicious anymore. It was tired. The question of a man who had been overlooked, passed over, and discarded so many times that being *chosen* felt like a trap.

"Because you were a baseball player," Gao Qiang said. "And baseball players understand something that most people don't: the season is long. You can lose today and still win the championship. The only thing that kills you is leaving the field."

Something shifted in Kojima's face. Not much — the swelling made expression difficult — but Gao Qiang saw it. A flicker. The ghost of the athlete he'd been before the injury, before the debt, before the basements and the duct-tape rings and the dirty towels.

"Who are you?" Kojima asked again. This time it wasn't a challenge. It was a genuine question.

"My name is Gao Qiang. I'm building something in this city — a team. A brotherhood. I need people who've been knocked down and chose to stand back up. That's you."

"What's the pay?"

"Room. Food. A purpose. And when the time comes, backup — the kind you've never had."

Kojima was quiet for a long time. The next fight started somewhere behind them. The crowd roared. Neither of them looked.

"I don't have anything left to lose," Kojima said finally. "You know that, right? You've seen me fight. You know I'm broke, broken, and one bad night away from —" He didn't finish the sentence. He didn't need to.

"I know."

"Then why —"

"Because I had nothing left to lose once too." Gao Qiang pulled down his surgical mask. Let Kojima see his face — young, Chinese, tired, determined. The face of someone who understood exactly what it meant to hit the bottom and keep moving. "And someone gave me a chance. Now I'm passing it on."

The system pulsed.

The golden flash — brighter this time, or maybe it just seemed that way in the dim basement. Kojima's body seized, every muscle locking for three terrible seconds, and then releasing with a shudder that rattled the bench beneath him.

When it passed, Kojima opened both eyes. The swollen one was still half-shut, but the look in them was transformed. The deadness was gone. In its place: fire. Not rage. Purpose. The kind that burns slow and doesn't go out.

"Big Brother," Kojima said. His voice was still a rasp, but it carried weight now.

"Welcome to the family." Gao Qiang offered his hand. Kojima took it. The grip was iron, even battered and bloodied.

"Can you walk?"

"I can do more than walk."

"Good. We're leaving. Kenji —" Gao Qiang caught his accountant's eye across the room. Kenji caught the signal and moved, reading the room in the way that made him invaluable. He settled something with the bartender — probably a tip for the inconvenience — and moved to the exit.

Mei was outside, leaning against a vending machine. When she saw Kojima — bleeding, limping, but walking under his own power — she didn't flinch. She pulled a clean towel from her bag and handed it to him without a word.

"Thanks," Kojima said, pressing it to his brow.

"Mei handles intelligence," Gao Qiang said. "She's the reason I knew where to find you."

Kojima looked at her — small, unassuming, the kind of woman you'd underestimate until she knew everything about you. "Respect," he said.

Mei nodded. "Don't bleed on my shoes."

They walked back through Kabukicho. Four people now. A Chinese student, a disgraced accountant, a waitress, and a broken fighter. The most dangerous thing in Kabukicho wasn't a gun or a knife or a yakuza boss.

It was a team.

Mei's intel said the collection ran on Thursday nights. Tomorrow was Friday. He had a week to plan.

But first — the money.

Kenji was already waiting at the kitchen table, the same notebook from Day 1 open in front of him. Two weeks of cramped handwriting, margin notes, and a running tally that GQ had never asked him to keep but that Kenji maintained with the precision of a man who found comfort in columns.

"We need to formalize this," Kenji said, not looking up. "Four people under one roof with irregular income streams. If we don't track it, we'll be short on rent and won't know why."

"Go ahead."

Kenji turned a fresh page and drew a line down the middle. On the left: Income. On the right: Household. At the bottom: Balance.

"The pool," he said. "Everyone contributes what they can. I'll track wages, expenses, and the running balance. Rent comes out first, then food and essentials. Anything left over is discretionary." He paused. "You make the decisions on distribution. I make sure the numbers are right."

Gao Qiang looked at the page. Clean. Simple. The kind of system that worked because a person maintained it, not because an algorithm enforced it.

"Starting balance?" Gao Qiang asked.

"Forty thousand in cash. Plus my wages come in Saturday — ninety thousand. Mei contributes what she can." Kenji wrote the numbers. "Projected pool after Saturday: roughly a hundred and three thousand."

The brotherhood had a budget.

He looked at Kojima, who was pressing the towel to his face and walking with a limp that was already improving.

A week. That was time to plan properly. Gao Qiang filed that away.

"Rest up, Kojima. I'll need you sharp next Thursday."

Kojima grinned. It split his lip open again, and blood ran down his chin, and he didn't care.

"Finally," he said. "Something I'm qualified for."
