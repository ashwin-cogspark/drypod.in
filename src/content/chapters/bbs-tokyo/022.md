Thursday, and the afternoon was going out of the room over the print shop.

The window gave its gray onto bare boards; under them the presses turned, their rhythm climbing into anyone's soles. The bag hung off the beam, still. Kojima Haruto stood beneath it, hands loose. Sato Kenjiro leaned in the corner by the window.

"The word went down this morning, through the regulars," Kojima said. "Book's open. A tenth off the winners. Door at eleven." He paused. "It came back up the same channel by noon. Twelve names asking after the room, near enough. Last week, eight came."

He put it down and left it there. "More men means more money on the floor. I hold the book. It isn't my purse either way."

Sato spoke from the corner, in his own order. "Last week, every face that came was a face I knew." A beat. "A word that travels brings faces I can't place. And names Kojima can't give back." He said nothing after that.

Gao Qiang weighed it at the wall. Eight faces walked out of the alley last Sunday carrying the address; twelve would walk out heavier. The weight and the money came in the same men.

"The door decides the size of the room," he said. "A name Kojima gives back gets in. A name that doesn't come back is a no — and your term stands, Sato. A man who asks twice after a no stays out." He looked at both of them. "The tenth stays a tenth. Nothing written."

Kojima nodded once. Sato turned his face back to the window. Nobody argued.

---

Sunday, and Gao Qiang came up the outside stair last, the cold working through the treads into his soles, his breath going up ahead of him.

The print shop's windows were dark below. Above, the bulb burned over the boards, the bag quiet on its chain, and Sato held the door cracked to the alley. At half past ten the noodle cart went by, tank rattling over the curb, and the alley stood empty after it. At eleven Sato opened the door and stood on the sill.

They came in ones and twos, coats steaming under the bulb. Each man gave a name at the sill and Kojima gave it back from the wall inside, and the man came up. Twelve came through, none of them loud.

Then a name crossed the sill and nothing crossed back. Gao Qiang came off the wall. The man on the step held himself still. Sato looked back inside; Kojima was not moving his head.

"No," Sato said. Not loud.

The man asked again, the same name slower, as though the first had been misheard. Sato said nothing this time. He stayed on the sill and let the quiet stand there with him.

The man's eyes went past him once — the bag, the boards — then he took the stair down, and the alley closed over him. One man at the wall glanced up from his coat and went back to it.

Before the first pairing, Kojima laid the rule once. "The house takes a tenth off the winners. Nobody owes the house at the end of a night. Last exchange at one." Nobody made him say it again.

Five pairings took the floor in turn. The rounds were short, the hands light, the falls given flat and taken rolling, the boards doing the work nobody's face had to. Nobody left marked. Before each round the stakes went into Kojima's hands and were paid back after; coming off, each winner counted out the tenth and folded it into his palm.

Coming off the floor warm, one winner asked Kojima for a turn.

"I hold the book," Kojima said.

The man took it in, nodded, and went back to the wall, and nobody asked anything behind him.

Gao Qiang stood at the wall and watched the hands — stakes moving, tenths moving, twelve faces that would each carry the alley home with them.

At one the coats came on without anyone calling it. The last men out gave Sato a nod at the sill and went down into the cold, and the alley stayed dark behind them.

Under the bulb, in the emptied room, Kojima said the night out loud once. "Twelve in. One turned away — asked twice, held. Five pairings, light, nobody marked. Winning bets, seventy thousand." He buttoned the roll against his ribs. "The house's tenth, seven thousand."

The three of them took the stair down and walked home with the roll, the streets gone quiet under the cold.

---

Sunday past two, and the lamp was on.

Murakami Kenji sat at the low table, home from the Goose since quarter past eleven, the notebook shut at his elbow. Kojima laid the roll out of his jacket and gave the night in his own order: "Twelve through the door. One turned away — asked twice, the no held. Five pairings, light, nobody marked. Rule laid once, stakes held and paid back. Winning bets, seventy thousand. The tenth, seven thousand."

Sato followed with the door, spare, in his own order. "One face I couldn't place. The name didn't come back. He asked a second time. I held the no. He went." Nothing after that.

"The ruling from Thursday, for the notebook," Gao Qiang said. "The door decides the size of the room. A second ask after a no shuts it."

Kenji opened the notebook before he touched the roll. "Saturday ran one envelope. The payday walk — the wage, from Nakamura's own hands. Her thirteen came in before eleven." The page showed the pool where last Sunday had left it, one hundred forty thousand nine hundred, and under it Saturday's two entries, the ninety and her thirteen. Two hundred forty-three thousand nine hundred.

He drew the roll to him and counted it where it lay, bills one at a time. "Seven thousand." He looked up. "Where does this money stand?"

"The room's," Gao Qiang said. "And the room's money is the pool's, same as last week."

The pencil ran the room's second line. "Two hundred forty-three thousand nine hundred, plus seven thousand." The stroke finished. "Two hundred fifty thousand nine hundred."

Kojima set the seven thousand against the number he had carried since the old room, once, flat. "Better than last week. Against the million and a half — still not a dent." Nobody offered it toward the debt, and he didn't ask. Kenji closed the notebook on it.

Gao Qiang set where the room would hold. "Twelve is the number. The door is the size of the room. That stands."

One thing the table could not enter. Gao Qiang thought of the man who had stood on that sill at eleven and gone down the stair with the alley behind him — the stair, the door, the hour. He did not know the face. He knew what the man would carry out of the alley: not the room, not the stakes. The no.
