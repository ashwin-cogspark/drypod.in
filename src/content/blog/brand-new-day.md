Today, I'm taking a pivot. 

Earlier today, I ran a self-discovery prompt in my AI Agent. It had access to all my Second Brain notes that I kept in Obsidian. 
I was hoping to find some hidden behaviour but, man, I was not ready for what actually came out of it.

It discovered an alarming trait of mine. 

I'm in a forever master loop.

```
1. New interest / identity ignites (dopamine, possibility)
   → 2. Build an elaborate plan/system/framework for it (feels productive)
      → 3. Execute the comfortable parts for 1–2 weeks
         → 4. Hit the uncomfortable part / a competing shiny object
            → 5. Drift; self-doubt voice activates ("you don't follow through")
               → 6. Recalibrate with a NEW, grander system (Step 2, escalated)
```

To an extent I was beginning to see signs of it myself. I'm always going after the next shiny object. I was beginning to see that there are many trackers piling on my desk (yes, I used to print them out for visibility).

I've decided to break the loop. 

Fortunately, the AI Agent also provided the one and only method to break it. 
It had concluded that being in this Master Loop had become my reflex.
The only thing that changes a reflex is repetition under awareness.

>The goal is to sequence this reflex: system-building becomes something that happens after shipping, as a tool, not instead of shipping.

And the best way is, to identify my primary goals and just walk towards it *without a system or plan or tracker*.

The goals that I chose are:
1. Continuing this blog (lol, I've literally just started it. My latest shiny object.)
2. Learning Python to improve fluency deliberately

And the reps that I need to do daily to overwrite my master loop is to **write a 200-word post or 20 lines of Python**.

So, *today*, I'm taking this pivot.

As recommended by my AI Agent.
